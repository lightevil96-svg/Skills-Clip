# Context, timing and decision records

## Evidence before treatment
Write a compact causal account: **trigger → response → consequence**. Add what
a first-time viewer needs to know. Link each clause to source intervals and
actual observations. Label interpretations CANDIDATE until checked. An avatar
expression can be rig motion; silence can be network delay. Neither alone
establishes embarrassment, anger or intent.

Compare candidates on meaning, not signal magnitude. Ask whether the payoff
survives without off-screen lore, whether necessary setup fits the brief and
whether a quieter candidate has a clearer complete story. Preserve discovery's
existing 0–12 score, but attach context debt and unknowns instead of turning a
high score into automatic selection.

## Timing procedure
1. Mark source onset of the meaningful word/action, reveal and response.
2. Listen/view context on both sides. For a joke, distinguish anticipation,
   semantic reveal and laughter onset. For emotion, preserve breath and recovery.
3. Place a cut where attention changes, not at a fixed time interval. Avoid
   chopping consonants or displaying the punchline before it is spoken.
4. Hold a reaction until its useful change finishes; trim repetitive tail only
   after preview. A pause that builds expectation is content, not dead air.
5. Compare nearby cut positions at native speed. Record the chosen boundary
   and what the shorter or longer alternative loses. Fixed millisecond recipes
   are experiments, not universal standards.

## Intensity scale

| Value | Treatment | Required reason |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Hold/direct cut; no added effect | Source already communicates the beat |
| 1 | Subtle reframe or one text emphasis | Direct attention without competing |
| 2 | Deliberate close-up or brief graphic | Clarify a specific contrast/reaction |
| 3 | Coordinated expressive accent | A strong beat benefits in preview |
| 4 | Exceptional stylized sequence | Explicit brief and evidence justify it; reduce immediately afterward |

Intensity measures the treatment, not whether the moment is good. There is no
quota or automatic escalation. Do not use total effect count as a quality score.

## Example comparison (invented scenario, not source dialogue)

An ASR draft describes a confident claim, a quiet pause, a visibly failed action
and a co-host's delayed laugh. A timer-based edit might remove the pause, zoom
every two seconds and attach an impact to every cut. The proposed decision
preserves the claim's necessary context, holds through the pause and failure,
then follows the actual laughing speaker. Choose no meme if the expression
already communicates the contrast. Caption only after source-audio verification.

Record the alternative: “earlier reaction cut” rejected because it hides the
result. If source inspection later shows the laugh precedes the failure, revise
the story; never move it later to validate the desired joke.
