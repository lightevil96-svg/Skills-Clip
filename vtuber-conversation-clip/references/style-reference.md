## Visual DNA

### Composition

- Portrait 9:16 throughout.
- The active VTuber is usually framed in a centered medium or medium-close shot. Face and upper body dominate the main picture.
- The source includes a narrow upper strip with stream/chat information at some moments, separated from the main character area by a pale horizontal divider. Names and emotes in this strip sometimes reinforce the joke.
- The clip alternates among three visible speaker presentations: a white-haired character, a red/pink-haired character, and a brown-haired character.
- The edit cuts to the current speaker, then occasionally widens to a two-character reaction shot when both expressions matter.
- Camera coverage is conversational and shot-based: one speaker gets a solo crop; simultaneous/overlapping speakers get one shared two-shot; when the turn changes, the camera changes with it.
- The original scene/background is retained. The edit does not replace it with a blurred duplicate background or a new graphic layout.
- The board/chat area is part of the original scene, not a permanent separate panel. It may receive a brief insert when it is relevant to the spoken explanation.

### Camera coverage — critical rule

The visual grammar is **speaker-following camera cuts**, not a fixed multi-panel composition:

1. If only one VTuber is speaking, frame that VTuber alone in a medium or medium-close crop. The other avatar should not remain as a permanent second panel.
2. If both VTubers speak simultaneously, overlap each other's lines, or their shared exchange is the point, use one continuous two-shot containing both avatars together.
3. When one speaker takes over, cut back to that speaker's solo crop at the next natural boundary.
4. A listener reaction may appear as a short cutaway only if the expression itself is the joke; then return to the speaker.
5. Never stack the full board/source scene in a top panel and an enlarged duplicate of the avatars in a bottom panel. Never use persistent split-screen, picture-in-picture, or duplicate views to fill a 9:16 canvas unless the user explicitly asks for that design.
6. For a landscape source, create shot-specific crops: solo crop for one speaker, wider shared crop for two speakers, and an occasional board insert only when the spoken content refers to it. Do not force the board to stay visible throughout the clip.

A render that keeps two separate panels on screen for the whole conversation fails this style even if its colors, borders, music, and vertical dimensions look polished.

### Subtitle treatment

Ordinary dialogue subtitles are placed over the image without an opaque pill or rectangular background.

- Typeface impression: rounded, bold sans-serif. The exact font cannot be identified reliably from the compressed raster; match the weight and rounded shape rather than claiming a specific font.
- Fill: speaker-specific color.
- Edge: thick light/white outline, with a subtle dark edge or shadow for separation from the scene.
- Alignment: centered.
- Placement: generally lower-middle, roughly 66–82% of the frame height, adjusted to avoid the face and relevant props.
- Length: short phrase fragments, normally one line and occasionally two. Captions change with the spoken phrase rather than remaining as a full transcript block.
- Translation/reading: the Japanese word in the reference is shown in its original script with a bracketed reading beneath it. Keep a reading distinct from a translation.

### Reaction graphics and visual emphasis

The reference uses a few highly specific visual gags:

1. **Image gag above the white-haired avatar (about 4.7–6.8 s):** a rectangular Indoapril sign/image appears above the character's head while the character speaks. It is a direct visual association with the conversation.
2. **Top chat/emote strip (several points):** usernames and emotes remain visible when they add context or a reaction.
3. **Confusion/loading symbols (about 10.0–11.6 s):** spinner-like marks appear above the red-haired and white-haired characters during a shared confused beat; the image is darker and shows both reactions.
4. **Laughter doodles (about 21.5–23.6 s):** repeated hand-drawn laughter marks float around the brown-haired character's head as the clip lands on the laugh.

Do not reuse the Indoapril logo or the exact stickers in unrelated clips. Recreate the *function* of each gag with a relevant image, emote, spinner, or doodle.

### Cut and transition language

- Most edits are direct jump cuts timed to the speaker or reaction.
- Visual beats tend to last around 1–3 seconds, but the duration varies with the line.
- The edit uses a short bright/blurred transition around 11.5–12.0 s to move into the next explanation beat.
- It does not use a transition on every cut.
- No persistent progress bar, countdown, opening title, or follow CTA is visible in this reference.
- The clip starts directly with a spoken observation and ends on the laugh/reaction.

## Anti-patterns observed in later adaptations

A later vertical adaptation used a static top/bottom layout: the full lesson board was placed in the upper panel while a zoomed view of both VTubers remained in the lower panel. This is **not** the reference style. It hides the conversational camera language and makes every moment look like a fixed collage.

For future edits, reject that layout during pre-render review. The camera must follow the active speaker with solo framing, then use a single shared two-shot only when both people are speaking/overlapping or their simultaneous reaction matters.

## What makes the style work

- The conversation is understandable even without a large title card.
- The audience can identify the active speaker immediately from the crop and caption color.
- The visual gag appears exactly when the spoken association becomes funny.
- A short shared-reaction shot communicates confusion faster than another line of explanation.
- The ending lets the character's expression and laughter carry the payoff.

Preserve these principles. Do not copy the exact joke, brand insert, character crop, or timestamp sequence into unrelated source footage.
